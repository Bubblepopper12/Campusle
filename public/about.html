About Campusle

The daily college guessing game

Campusle is a free puzzle game for anyone who loves college: high school students touring schools, college students repping their campus, and fans who know every program on a Saturday slate. Each day there is one mystery answer, the same for every player. You get six guesses to find it, and every guess gives you clues that point you closer.

It works like Wordle, but instead of letters you are comparing real facts: where a school is, how big it is, how selective it is, what it costs, how old it is, and who it plays. When you finish, you can share a spoiler-free emoji grid with friends to see who solved it fastest.

The daily games

  • 🎓 Classic picks a well-known U.S. college. Clues compare distance and direction on the map, undergraduate enrollment, acceptance rate, in-state tuition, founding year, and athletic conference.
  • 🧠 Classic · Hard uses the same clues, but the answer is a smaller or lesser-known school: liberal arts colleges, regional universities, HBCUs, art and engineering schools, and service academies. It is a good way to discover colleges you may never have heard of.
  • 🏈 Football picks an FBS college football program. Clues compare location, football conference, stadium capacity, the first season of varsity football, claimed national championships, and team colors.
  • ⭐ Players picks a famous college football player, past or present. Clues compare their school, position, final college season, whether they won the Heisman Trophy, national titles won while they were on the roster, and jersey number.

Why play

Campusle is quick (most games take two to five minutes), it resets at midnight in your own time zone, and it quietly teaches you about American higher education. After a few weeks you will know which schools are in which conference, which states have the oldest universities, and how acceptance rates and tuition vary from one campus to the next.

Where the information comes from

Enrollment, tuition, acceptance rates, location and public or private status come from the U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard, the government's official college data. Founding years come from Wikidata, with a few hand corrections where it lists a merger or renaming date instead. Athletic conferences and mascots are kept current by hand for the 2026 season, and football and player details are compiled by hand from public records of each program. If you spot something that looks wrong, it will be corrected in a future update.
